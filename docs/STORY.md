# Doodle Voyager - the story

Doodle Voyager is a fan sequel to Doodle Shooter (doodleshooter.vercel.app, in-game title
"Doodle District"). Everything below is built on that game's own canon: a world drawn in blue
ballpoint on lined notebook paper, the maps Doodle District ("streets, rooftops and fire
escapes") and Doodle Mexico, the seven red enemy types (Grunt, Rusher, Heavy, Sniper,
Shieldbearer, Ink Bomb, Paper Wasp), the three bosses (The Doodler, The Eraser, The Inkblot)
and its words for losing: ERASED, FELL OFF THE PAGE, "they are crawling off the page".
Sources are listed at the end (research run 1790286419, 2026-09-24).

## The premise

Doodle District held. Survivors cleared wave after wave, and every red guy that crawled off
the page got ERASED. So the red guys stopped crawling. They climbed - over the red margin line,
off the edge of the notebook, and out into the real sky, where every star, every catalogued
exoplanet and ten thousand galaxies are waiting. They call themselves the Red Margin now,
after the line they crossed.

Out here the paper is gone, and something odd happens to ink in a vacuum: it glows. That is
why Voyager is neon where Doodle Shooter was blue on white.

## The player

You are the Survivor: the doodle who got ERASED more often than anyone in Doodle District and
kept coming back from the lobby. When the red guys left the page, you folded your pencil case
into a ship and followed them. Spiral Bound Galactic calls you "a valued customer". The Red
Margin calls you "the one with the pencil case".

## Factions

**The Red Margin (the red guys).** The same seven types from Doodle District, promoted.
Paper Wasps fly the fighter swarms. Shieldbearers fly screens around the capital ships. Snipers
took the turret beams (the lasers are still dodgeable - tradition). Heavies captain the
dreadnoughts and carriers. Grunts crew the saucers. Rushers fly the attack runs. Ink Bombs
drift as mines. In Doodle District they came in waves; out here they come in wings, in
formation, with a plan.

**Spiral Bound Galactic.** The megacorp that holds it all together, terms and conditions
bind. It owns the shipyards, the Bazaar, the fuel pumps and the Billboard Worlds programme,
which rents out whole planet faces as advertising space. It sells fuel to you and to the Red
Margin at the same price, which it calls "fair".

**The bosses.** The three from Doodle Shooter lead the Red Margin, one per act; five
lieutenants hold the great clusters (new for Voyager).

| Boss | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| The Doodler | Milky Way (Act 1) | Draws new red guys faster than you can erase them; now draws capital ships. |
| The Eraser | Andromeda (Act 2) | Rubs out stations, pumps and moons. "One shot, one erasure" was your line; now it is his business model. |
| The Inkblot | The Great Attractor (Act 3) | Sprays. Sits behind the Milky Way's own dust and leaks dark ink toward the place every galaxy is drifting. |
| The Scribbler | Virgo Cluster | Scribbles over M87's glow so nobody can read the map. |
| The Smudge | Coma Cluster | Hides in the missing mass. Hard to see, easy to feel. |
| The Hole Punch | Fornax Cluster | Punches neat round holes in fleets. Collects the dots. |
| The Paper Cut | Perseus-Pisces | Small, fast, and it really, really hurts. |
| The Staple Remover | Hydra-Centaurus | Pries convoys apart. Jaws on both sides. |

## Why fuel is a racket

Three fuels (ION, PLASMA, DEUTERIUM), three nozzles, three patents, all Spiral Bound
Galactic's. Every pump is licensed to sell one to three of them, never guaranteed to be yours.
Converters exist but are "not recommended by the manufacturer". The annoyance is working as
intended - it is the business model. (The joke is the same one as canned air sold by the
people causing the shortage.)

Station announcer lines (cheerful, automated, useless):
- "Welcome, valued customer. This pump does not serve your fuel type. Please enjoy the music."
- "Spiral Bound Fuels: not the closest pump. Not the cheapest pump. A pump."
- "Your call is important to us. Your ship is not."
- "Because this message is recorded, any praise of your piloting is a guess."

## Three-act arc

**Act 1 - Off the Page (the Milky Way).** You wake up at Sol Pumps in Earth orbit. The
tutorial happens here: flying, the throttle, docking, discovering that the pump does not serve
your fuel, and your first wing of Paper Wasps in a clean V. Red Margin zones spread across the
galaxy. You chase them inward, past the planets carrying Spiral Bound Galactic ads, to The
Doodler's capital near the galactic centre, where the black hole pulls at everything including
you. Boss: The Doodler.

**Act 2 - Billboard Worlds (the Local Group, the Bazaar, Andromeda).** At the Bazaar, a shop
cluster the size of a dwarf galaxy between the Milky Way and Andromeda, you learn that Spiral
Bound Galactic rents out every planet the Red Margin "liberates", and sells both sides their
fuel. The Eraser is rubbing out Andromeda's stations to fix the odds of the big collision:
astronomers say it is now a coin flip, and the Red Margin does not like coin flips. Boss: The
Eraser.

**Act 3 - Immeasurable Heaven (Laniakea and the Great Attractor).** The trail leads out through
the great clusters, one lieutenant each, to the Zone of Avoidance behind the Milky Way's own
disk, where the Great Attractor sits and where the Inkblot is leaking. If the leak reaches the
Attractor, every galaxy drifting toward it drifts into one blot. Boss: The Inkblot. When it
dries, it glows like everything else out here. Somewhere a notebook closes. The sky keeps going
(procedural galaxies after the credits).

## Intro crawl (shown at the start of every NEW GAME, skippable) - 87 words

> Doodle District held. Wave after wave the red guys crawled off the page, and wave after wave
> they got ERASED.
> So they stopped crawling. They climbed.
> Past the red margin there is no paper, only sky: every real star, every charted world, ten
> thousand galaxies. Out here, the ink glows.
> The Red Margin is drawing its zones across all of it. Spiral Bound Galactic is renting the
> planets out as billboards. The pumps never take your fuel.
> Fold up the Pencil Case. You're going off the page.

## Codex (12 entries, unlocked in play)

1. **Doodle District.** Streets, rooftops and fire escapes in blue ballpoint on lined paper.
   Survivors counted waves; the red guys counted erasures. Nobody counted what was past the
   margin. *(Unlocks: new game.)*
2. **The Red Margin.** Grunt, Rusher, Heavy, Sniper, Shieldbearer, Ink Bomb, Paper Wasp: the
   same seven, now flying in wings with a leader and a plan. Kill the leader and watch the plan
   fall apart. *(Unlocks: first wing destroyed.)*
3. **The Doodler.** Draws red guys. Now draws capital ships, which is worse. Fights by
   summoning and throwing. *(Unlocks: Act 1 boss seen.)*
4. **The Eraser.** Rubs things out - stations, pumps, a moon once. Charges when cornered.
   *(Unlocks: Act 2 boss seen.)*
5. **The Inkblot.** Sprays. Leaks. Waits behind the dust where nobody looks.
   *(Unlocks: Act 3 boss seen.)*
6. **Spiral Bound Galactic.** Owner of the shipyards, the pumps, the Bazaar and the Billboard
   Worlds programme. Slogan: "We hold it all together. Terms and conditions bind."
   *(Unlocks: first docking.)*
7. **The Fuel Racket.** Three fuels, three nozzles, three patents. Converters are "not
   recommended by the manufacturer". *(Unlocks: first refused pump.)*
8. **Billboard Worlds.** Planet faces rented by the hemisphere. The ad turns with the planet;
   the planet was not consulted. *(Unlocks: first planet ad seen.)*
9. **Why the Ink Glows.** On paper, ballpoint is blue and flat. In vacuum, it glows. Spiral
   Bound Galactic cannot explain this, so it sells it. *(Unlocks: first launch.)*
10. **The Coin Flip.** The Milky Way and Andromeda were supposed to collide in 4 to 5 billion
    years. A 2025 study ran 100,000 simulations and found about a 50 per cent chance they merge
    within 10 billion years - and only about 2 per cent for the old head-on crash in 4 to 5.
    The Large Magellanic Cloud and Triangulum tip the odds. The Red Margin would like to tip
    them harder. *(Unlocks: reach Andromeda.)*
11. **Laniakea and the Clusters.** Our home supercluster, named in 2014 - "immeasurable heaven"
    - about 500 million light-years across and about 100,000 galaxies. Nearby clusters: Virgo
    (about 54 million light-years, around 1,300 to 2,000 galaxies, M87 at its heart), Fornax
    (about 62 million), Coma (about 330 million, where "missing mass" first showed up - dark
    matter, NASA's "stuff in space that has gravity, but it is invisible"), Perseus-Pisces
    (about 250 million, just outside Laniakea). *(Unlocks: leave the Local Group.)*
12. **The Great Attractor.** Something heavy behind the Milky Way's own disk, in the Zone of
    Avoidance toward Norma, about 150 to 250 million light-years away. The Local Group drifts
    toward it. So does the Inkblot's leak. *(Unlocks: Act 3.)*

## How the story is delivered (design rules from the research)

- The intro crawl plays at the start of every NEW GAME (not on continue), is skippable with
  any key or button, and stays under 90 words. Half-Life's opening tells the setting with no
  cutscene and keeps the controls tutorial separate; Voyager does the crawl first, then puts
  the tutorial inside Act 1 at Sol Pumps.
- The tutorial is also story, the way Portal's test chambers are: the cheerful Spiral Bound
  Galactic station announcer teaches docking and the fuel racket in the same breath. Skippable,
  replayable from the menu.
- Everything else is environmental: planet ads, announcer lines, ship descriptions (the
  Hollow Knight / FTL approach of a sentence of flavour per item), and codex entries that
  unlock on events, like the Outer Wilds ship log, instead of text dumps.
- Tone: sincere corporate cheer about something absurd; slogans that undercut their own
  product; the corporation monetising something that should be free (fuel, a view of a
  planet) - the patterns behind Aperture, Hyperion, Spacer's Choice, Sirius Cybernetics and
  Perri-Air. No real brands in the ads.
